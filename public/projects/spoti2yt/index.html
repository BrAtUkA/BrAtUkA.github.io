Why I built it

Music gets shared as YouTube links and as Spotify links, and they rarely match the app you use. Finding the same song on the other platform is a small, annoying job that keeps coming up.

spoti2yt does it with one keypress. Copy a link from either platform, press the hotkey, and the matching link replaces it on your clipboard, with a sound and a small notification. It started as a Python script and was rewritten in Rust so the matching is more reliable, it uses no CPU while waiting, and it has a real window.

Ctrl+Shift+Y
Spotify → YouTube
Ctrl+Shift+U
YouTube → Spotify
Ctrl+Shift+J
The song playing on Spotify → YouTube
Ctrl+Shift+P
A playlist → every track link
Ctrl+Shift+L
Add the copied song to Liked Songs
Ctrl+Shift+T
Song details plus its YouTube link

If the clipboard doesn’t hold the right kind of link, the hotkey does nothing, on purpose. Every hotkey can be changed or turned off.

The matcher

This is the core of the project. YouTube is full of near-copies of every song: lyric videos, live versions, sped-up edits, covers, fan uploads. Comparing titles isn’t enough, because the wrong video often has the closest title.

So every result gets a score out of 1, built from three parts: how close the title is, whether the channel matches the artist, and how close the length is. Official “Artist - Topic” channels get a bonus. Results with words the Spotify track doesn’t have, like live or sped up, get a penalty. A result has to clear a confidence floor, 0.60 by default, to be used at all.

Going the other way, from YouTube to Spotify, the artist is harder to know. The app collects guesses from the channel name, the uploader, YouTube Music’s own data and both halves of a title like “Artist - Song”, and tries each one. That’s what lets a lyric video posted by a random channel still find the right Spotify track.

Every confident match is saved in a local SQLite database, in both directions. Converting the same song again is instant, whichever way you go.

Playlists

Paste a playlist link, or pick one of your own, to see every track. Convert all to YouTube resolves them a few at a time and shows the score for each as it lands.

When a track can’t be matched confidently, you can review it: see the nearest results with their scores, preview them, paste your own link, or ask it to search wider. A picked match is saved, so it’s right from then on.

Around the edges

  • Now playing. A bar along the bottom controls Spotify: play, pause, skip, shuffle, repeat, seek, volume and like. Skipping feels instant because it already knows the next song in the queue. Controls need Spotify Premium; free accounts get a read-only bar.
  • Private by design. You sign in through your own free Spotify developer app, using the OAuth PKCE flow, so no shared app or server sits in the middle. The sign-in token is kept in the Windows Credential Manager. There’s no telemetry.
  • Nothing extra to install. The installer includes yt-dlp, which does the YouTube searching, and fetches WebView2 if it’s missing.
