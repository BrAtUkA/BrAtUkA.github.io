Why I built it

Camera moves animated by hand look like they were animated by hand. A real handheld camera has small drifts and corrections that are hard to fake with keyframes. Studios get that feel from motion-capture rigs. I wanted it from the phone in my pocket.

Blend-CamMoCap is two halves: an Android app and a Blender add-on. They find each other on your Wi-Fi with a QR code. Tap Go live, and Blender’s camera follows the phone. Hit Record, move, hit it again, and the move is baked into normal keyframes you can trim and edit like any other animation.

There’s no account, no cloud and no subscription. The phone and the PC talk directly, and nothing leaves the room.

How it works

The hard part is that Wi-Fi is unreliable, and a camera that stutters or jumps is useless. So the live preview and the take you keep travel separately.

  • The preview only cares about now. The phone sends its position as small UDP packets, a hundred times a second. Each one is the whole current pose, not a change, so a lost packet means a brief freeze rather than a jump. Blender applies the newest pose at 120 Hz.
  • The take doesn’t depend on the Wi-Fi. While you record, the phone also saves every sample to its own storage. When you stop, it uploads that file in full and the add-on resamples it at the scene’s frame rate. A glitch mid-take only affects what you saw, never what you keep.
  • The viewfinder shows the real shot. Blender draws its camera off screen and streams the frames to the phone. It keeps working when Blender is minimised or you’re in another editor, because it isn’t a copy of your screen.

Shooting with it

  • Rotation, or the full move. By default the phone controls where the camera points. Turn on 6DOF and ARCore tracks the phone’s position too, so walking moves the camera through the scene. If tracking gets poor, the phone says why (“point at a detailed surface”) and holds still instead of lurching.
  • Lens on the phone. A zoom dial under the viewfinder sets Blender’s focal length, and a zoom during a take is recorded with the move.
  • Shoot away from the PC. Takes can be recorded on the phone alone and applied later. The phone keeps a searchable library of takes you can rename, re-apply or delete.
  • Shoot over your animation. The timeline plays while you record, so you can follow a moving subject by hand. Every take is kept; nothing is overwritten.

Built to be tested

Phone sensors and Blender use different ideas of which way is up, and it’s easy for one side to drift out of step with the other. Both halves are tested against the same set of shared example data, so the Android code and the Blender code have to agree exactly.

  • A fake phone. A small script pretends to be the app, so the add-on can be developed and tested without a handset.
  • A Motion Lab. It measures lag, jitter and how faithfully a take is baked, and fails the test run if any of them get worse.
  • Several Blenders. The add-on is tested headless on Blender 4.5, 5.0 and 5.1.

What’s next

The wire protocol is written down and versioned, so other clients can be checked against the same test data. An iOS app is the obvious next one.
