Why I built it

At night, a white PDF page is the brightest thing in the room. Browser dark-mode extensions only change how a PDF looks while it’s open in that browser, and most online converters want you to upload your file to their server.

I found a small open-source converter by Chizkiyahu that did the job in the browser, but it was slow, had one look, and turned every page into a flat picture you couldn’t search. I forked it and rewrote most of it. It is now my most-starred project.

How it works

Everything happens on your device. The page is drawn, recoloured, and put back together into a new PDF without a server ever seeing it.

  • The colours change on the graphics card. A theme is a colour matrix (an SVG feColorMatrix filter) applied to the whole page at once. Firefox can’t use filters on a canvas, so it gets a slower fallback that does the same maths in JavaScript.
  • Photos are left alone. Before recolouring, the tool reads the page’s drawing commands to find where each image sits. It saves those pixels and puts them back afterwards, so photos and diagrams keep their real colours.
  • The text stays real. A converted page is an image, but the original text goes back on top as an invisible layer. Search, select and copy all still work.
  • Pages are done in parallel. It converts as many pages at once as your processor has cores. That, plus the GPU filter, is where most of the speed-up comes from.

Themes

There are sixteen themes, from plain inversion to Dracula, Nord, Solarized and Tokyo Night. You can also pick any colour, and switch from dark to light output if you want to go the other way.

The details

  • Several files at once. Drop in a stack of PDFs and they come back as one download.
  • Your choice of size. Resolution goes from 0.5× to 4×, and JPEG quality from 50 to 100 percent, so you can trade file size for sharpness.
  • A separate invert tool. For a true colour inversion, for example before printing, there’s a second tool next to the main one.
  • Guides. Fourteen short guides cover dark mode for PDFs in Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Windows, iPhone, iPad, Android, Acrobat and Google Drive.
